Background: the expansions

This page summarizes what the package computes; the paper gives the derivations, the error bounds and the numerical evidence.

Setting

$X$ is a random vector of dimension $d$ with cumulant generating function $K(t) = \log E\,e^{t'X}$, finite on an open set containing the origin. The large parameter $n$ (a sample size, degrees of freedom, ...) is absorbed in $K$: for the mean of $n$ observations, $K_n(t) = nK(t/n)$ (sample_mean). The saddlepoint $\tau$ at a point $x$ solves $K'(\tau) = x$, and $K''(\tau)$ is the covariance matrix of the exponentially tilted distribution.

Densities (Theorem 3)

\[f(x) = \frac{e^{K(\tau) - \tau'x}}{(2\pi)^{d/2}\,|K''(\tau)|^{1/2}} \Bigl\{1 + \sum_{m \ge 1} n^{-m} b_m(\tau)\Bigr\},\]

where $b_m$ is a finite combination of the Taylor coefficients of $K$ at $\tau$ of orders three to $2m + 2$ and of multivariate Hermite polynomials at zero. The expansion of order $M$ keeps $m \le M - 1$ and has relative error $O(n^{-M})$. In one dimension this is Daniels's expansion.

Orthant probabilities (Theorem 1)

\[P(X \ge x) = e^{K(\tau) - \tau'x + \tau'K''(\tau)\tau/2} \sum_{m \ge 0} n^{-m/2} \sum_{\nu} a_{m,\nu}(\tau)\, I(\nu),\]

with coefficients $a_{m,\nu}$ built from the same Taylor coefficients and integrals $I(\nu) = \int_{w \ge 0} He_\nu(w; K'')\,\phi(w + K''\tau; K'')\,dw$ of Hermite polynomials against a normal density over the orthant. The expansion of order $M$ keeps $m \le 2M - 1$ and has relative error $O(n^{-M})$, uniformly for saddlepoints in compact subsets of the closed positive orthant $\tau \ge 0$, so including points on the boundary where some components of the saddlepoint vanish. The term $m = 0$ is the approximation of Kolassa (2003). In one dimension the expansion is that of Hauschildt and Robinson, whose first-order term has the same accuracy as the Lugannani-Rice formula.

The integrals $I(\nu)$ are evaluated in whitened coordinates (Appendix B of the paper): for $Y = L^{-1}X$, $K'' = LL'$, the Hermite polynomials factor into univariate ones and the terms cancel far less. In two dimensions $I(\nu)$ is computed by Gauss quadrature (or by the exact recursion of Lemma 2 in variable precision), in three by Gauss quadrature on a conditioned grid, and in four or more by randomized quasi-Monte Carlo after Genz's sequential conditioning.

Saddlepoints with negative components (Section 3.3, Theorem 2)

When $\tau$ has negative components, the probability is written by inclusion-exclusion as a signed sum of orthant probabilities of lower-dimensional marginals and of reflected vectors, each with a saddlepoint in its closed positive orthant, and each is expanded. Theorem 2 of the paper shows that this preserves the relative error bound under mild conditions; the paper also shows that the direct evaluation of the formula at such a saddlepoint has the wrong exponential order.

Ratios (Theorem 4)

For $R = X_1/X_2$ with $X_2 > 0$, $P(R \ge r) = P(X_1 - rX_2 \ge 0)$ is a univariate tail probability with cumulant generating function $K_W(s) = K(s, -rs)$, and the density is the Rice expansion of $(2\pi i)^{-1}\int e^{K_W(s)}\,\partial_2K(s, -rs)\,ds$ through the saddlepoint $K_W'(\hat s) = 0$.

Studentized means

For $Q = X/\sqrt{Y}$ with $Y > 0$ the event $X \le q\sqrt{Y}$ is not linear, and the inversion formulas of Bao, Broda and Kan, Inversion formulas and saddlepoint expansions for Studentized means, are double integrals, $f_Q(q) = (2\pi i)^{-2}\iint e^{K(s,t)}\,2J_2(qs, t)\,dt\,ds$ and $P(Q > q) = (2\pi i)^{-2}\iint e^{K(s,t)}\,2J_1(qs, t)\,dt\,ds/s$, with kernels $J_k(b, t) = \int_0^\infty v^k e^{-bv - tv^2}dv$ whose exponent is $q^2s^2/(4t)$ plus Gaussian terms. The density is the amplitude expansion of the double integral about the saddlepoint of $\psi(s, t) = K(s, t) + q^2s^2/(4t)$, and the distribution function is obtained by expanding the $t$-integral by Laplace's method, which leaves a univariate integral of Hauschildt-Robinson type in $s$ with the exponent $\Psi(s) = \psi(s, t(s))$, $\psi_t(s, t(s)) = 0$, and an amplitude $H_0(s)$ with its own Rice corrections. The Hessian of $\psi$ is indefinite where $\hat t < 0$, which is the whole lower tail when $E(X) = 0$; the expansions are the same on both sides.

Accuracy in practice

In the examples of the paper (means of five to ten observations of exponential variables, pairs of quadratic forms in normal variables with ten to sixty degrees of freedom, the diagonal of a Wishart matrix with five degrees of freedom) the first-order approximations have relative errors of one to thirty percent, order two reduces them to $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-4}$, and order five to $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-9}$. Each doubling of $n$ divides the error of order $M$ by about $2^M$.
