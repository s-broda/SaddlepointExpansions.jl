The derivative interface

The engine behind tailprob and density can be used directly with derivatives of the cumulant generating function supplied by the user, for models whose derivatives are available in closed form or by a recursion (large quadratic forms, for instance, where the trace recursion of qf_Kmulti is faster than differentiating a log-determinant). These are the functions of the replication material of the paper, with the same names and conventions.

The cumulant generating function is a function Kfun(nu, t) returning the derivative of multi-index order nu (a tuple of d integers; nu = (0, …, 0) gives $K(t)$) at the vector t, and NaN outside the domain. A CGF can be turned into one with SaddlepointExpansions._kfun(K, maxdeg) (internal).

Whitened evaluation: spa_bi and spa_mv accept a function Kwhite(nu, u, T) returning the derivatives of $K_Y(u) = K(Tu)$; without it, the whitened Taylor coefficients are obtained by substitution, and coef_err_pdf, coef_err_cdf in the result estimate the digits lost (the CGF interface always supplies Kwhite).

Ready-made univariate distributions

ncx2_spa, ncf_spa, dncf_spa (noncentral chi-squared, singly and doubly noncentral $F$) return the expansions of the distribution function, upper tail and density, and ncx2_exact, gamma_exact, dncf_exact_dp, bqfcc_exact, mvcc_exact give exact values to compare with.

First-order approximations of the literature

kl_bivar (Kolassa and Li 2010) and wang_bivar (Wang 1990) evaluate the two bivariate first-order approximations the paper compares with, from a Kfun. The first-order term of tailprob is the approximation of Kolassa (2003).
