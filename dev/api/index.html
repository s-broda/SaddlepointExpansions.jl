API reference

Cumulant generating functions and the high-level interface

SaddlepointExpansions.CGF — Type
CGF(K, d; domain=nothing)

A cumulant generating function $K$ of a random vector of dimension d, given as a Julia function of one argument, a vector t of length d (also when d = 1: write t[1]). K must be written generically: it is evaluated on vectors of TaylorN series to generate derivatives, and on BigFloat values for variable precision, so it should use only arithmetic and the elementary functions (exp, log, sqrt, powers, sum, matrix products with constant matrices, ...), without type assertions or calls that force Float64.

Outside the domain of $K$ the function may throw a DomainError (as log and sqrt of negative arguments do) or return a non-finite value; both are understood as "outside the domain". domain, an optional predicate t -> Bool, states the domain explicitly, which is cheaper and more robust than relying on exceptions when the formula for $K$ is finite outside its domain (for instance $-\log(1-t)$ for $t > 1$ is a DomainError, but $1/(1-t)$ is not).

The keyword taylor supplies the Taylor polynomials in closed form instead of by automatic differentiation: a function (t, N, M) -> TaylorN returning the Taylor polynomial of degree N in v of $K(t + Mv)$ (M = nothing for the identity; M may be rectangular), or nothing outside the domain, built from a coefficient table with taylor_from_coefficients. The library uses it where closed forms are faster (quadratic_forms_cgf, by the trace recursion), and the transformations carry it along.

A CGF is callable: K(t) evaluates the function. See derivatives, saddlepoint, tailprob, density, and the transformations reflect, marginal, linear_combination, sample_mean, shift and + (sum of independent vectors).

Example

K = CGF(t -> -3*log(1 - t[1]) - 2*log(1 - t[2]) - log(1 - t[1] - t[2]), 2;
        domain = t -> t[1] < 1 && t[2] < 1 && t[1] + t[2] < 1)

SaddlepointExpansions.density — Method
density(K::CGF, x; order=3, kwargs...) -> (; f, tau, status, info)

Saddlepoint expansions of orders 1:order of the density at x of the random vector with cumulant generating function K: f[M] keeps the terms $m ≤ M-1$ of the expansion in powers of $n^{-1}$ and has relative error $O(n^{-M})$ (Theorem 3 of the paper; Daniels's expansion when d = 1). The expansion is a finite sum of Hermite polynomials and needs no integration, in any dimension. tau is the saddlepoint, status is "ok" or a warning about the evaluation, and info the result of the underlying routine.

In three or more dimensions the terms of high orders can cancel by many orders of magnitude; the cancellation indicator is monitored and the computation is repeated automatically in BigFloat when it exceeds $10^6$ (refine = true, the default; see spa_mv). Keywords are passed on to spa_bi (d = 2) and spa_mv (d ≥ 3).

source
SaddlepointExpansions.ratio_density — Method
ratio_density(K::CGF, r; order=3, general=false) -> (; f, shat, status, info)

Saddlepoint expansions of orders 1:order of the joint density at r of the vector of ratios $R = (X_1/Y_1, …, X_d/Y_d)$, $d$ = length(r), with positive denominators $Y_j > 0$. K is the cumulant generating function of $(X_1, …, X_d, Y_1, …, Y_d)$ (dimension $2d$) or, for a common denominator $Y$, of $(X_1, …, X_d, Y)$ (dimension $d + 1$). For a scalar r (one ratio, K of dimension two) the expansion is the Rice (Laplace-type) expansion of $f_R(r) = (2πi)^{-1}∫\exp\{K_W(s)\}\,g(s)\,ds$, $K_W(s) = K(s, -rs)$, $g(s) = ∂_2K(s, -rs)$, through the saddlepoint $K_W'(\hat s) = 0$ (Theorem 4 and its corollary in the paper); for a vector it is Theorem 4 with the amplitude expansion of Corollary 1, in whitened coordinates, with every Taylor coefficient taken from one Taylor polynomial of $K(s, -r∘s + τ)$ in $(u, τ)$. f[M] has relative error $O(n^{-M})$; shat is the saddlepoint of $K_W$. general = true uses the vector code also for $d = 1$ (for checks).

source
SaddlepointExpansions.ratio_tailprob — Method
ratio_tailprob(K::CGF, r; order=3, lower=false, signs=nothing, kwargs...) -> (; F, shat, status, info)

Saddlepoint expansions of orders 1:order of $P(R ≥ r)$ for the vector of ratios $R = (X_1/Y_1, …, X_d/Y_d)$ with positive denominators (K as in ratio_density; a scalar r for one ratio): the probability equals $P(W ≥ 0)$ for $W = X - r∘Y$, and tailprob is applied to $W$ at $0$ (Theorem 4 of the paper; the univariate expansion for one ratio). lower = true gives $P(R ≤ r)$, signs a mixed corner, and the other keywords are passed to tailprob. shat is the saddlepoint of $K_W$.

SaddlepointExpansions.saddlepoint — Method
saddlepoint(K::CGF, x; tau0=nothing) -> (tau, info)

The saddlepoint $K'(τ) = x$ (Newton's method with backtracking on the convex objective $K(τ) - τ'x$; see spa_saddle for the stopping rules and the contents of info). An error is raised if the iteration does not converge. For d = 1, x may be a scalar, and tau is then a scalar.

SaddlepointExpansions.tailprob — Method
tailprob(K::CGF, x; order=3, lower=false, signs=nothing, kwargs...)
    -> (; F, tau, status, info)

Saddlepoint expansions of orders 1:order of the orthant probability $P(X ≥ x)$ (componentwise) for the random vector with cumulant generating function K, at the point x (a vector of length d; a scalar when d = 1). F[M] is the expansion of order M, which keeps the terms of orders $n^{-m/2}$, $m ≤ 2M-1$, of the expansion in powers of $n^{-1/2}$, and has relative error $O(n^{-M})$ uniformly for saddlepoints in compact subsets of the closed positive orthant (Theorem 1 of the paper; the "$n$" is absorbed in K). F[1] is the first-order approximation of Kolassa (2003) in two or more dimensions and that of Hauschildt and Robinson in one.

  • lower = true gives $P(X ≤ x)$, and signs (a vector of $±1$) any mixed corner, e.g. signs = [1, -1] for $P(X_1 ≥ x_1, X_2 ≤ x_2)$; the components are reflected (reflect) and the result is an upper orthant probability of the reflected vector.
  • When the saddlepoint has negative components the expansion is applied through the inclusion-exclusion identity of Section 3.3 of the paper (lower-dimensional marginal terms with their own saddlepoints), which keeps the relative error bound.
  • tau is the saddlepoint of K at x. status is "ok" or a description of a problem in the evaluation (see spa_bi and spa_mv); info is the full result of the underlying routine, with the cancellation indicators, quadrature checks and saddlepoint diagnostics.

The derivatives of K are generated by Taylor-mode differentiation at the saddlepoint, directly in the whitened coordinates in which the terms are evaluated. The computation runs in the floating-point type of x; with BigFloat arguments (inside setprecision) everything, the differentiation included, is done in that precision. In three dimensions the integrals $I(ν)$ are computed by Gauss quadrature and in four or more by randomized quasi-Monte Carlo (info.err is then a standard error); see spa_mv for the keywords (nG, nqmc, ...), which are passed on, and spa_orthant2 for those of the bivariate case (nG, logscale for probabilities below the double-precision range, ...).

SaddlepointExpansions.studentized_density — Method
studentized_density(K::CGF, q; order=3, saddle=nothing) -> (; f, saddle, status, info)

Saddlepoint expansions of orders 1:order of the density at q of $Q = X/\sqrt{Y}$, $Y > 0$, from the joint cumulant generating function K of $(X, Y)$ (Theorem 2 of Bao, Broda and Kan): f[M] keeps the terms $m ≤ M - 1$ of the expansion in powers of $n^{-1}$ and has relative error $O(n^{-M})$ (the "$n$" is absorbed in K, see sample_mean). With $\psi(s, t) = K(s, t) + q^2s^2/(4t)$ and the saddlepoint $(\hat s, \hat t, \hat v)$ of studentized_saddlepoint,

\[f_Q(q) ≈ \frac{\hat v^2 e^{\psi(\hat s, \hat t)}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\{\hat t\,|\psi''(\hat s, \hat t)|\}^{1/2}} \Big\{1 + \sum_{m ≥ 1} b_m(h_0) + b_{m-1}(h_1)\Big\},\]

the amplitude expansion of a bivariate integral with amplitude $h_0 + h_1 = (s/\hat s)^2(t/\hat t)^{-5/2} + (t/\hat t)^{-3/2}/(2\hat t\hat v^2)$; the Hessian $\psi''$ is indefinite where $\hat t < 0$, and the Hermite moments are evaluated in the coordinates of a complex factorization $\psi'' = LL'$. The terms of high order cancel by many orders of magnitude when $|\hat t|$ is large; info.kappa is the cancellation indicator, and with refine = true (the default) a Float64 computation whose indicator exceeds $10^6$ is repeated in BigFloat with a precision chosen from the indicator (info.refined is then the number of bits). saddle may supply the saddlepoint ((; s, t, v)).

source
SaddlepointExpansions.studentized_locus — Method
studentized_locus(K::CGF, s, tguess) -> (; t, v, q)

The point of the saddlepoint locus $s K_s + 2 t K_t = 0$ with abscissa s, by Newton's method in $t$ from tguess, with $v = K_t^{1/2}$ and the value $q = K_s/v$ whose saddlepoint it is. Eliminating $q$ and $v$ from the saddlepoint equations shows that the saddlepoints for all $q$ lie on this curve through the origin, so that a value of $\hat s$ determines the saddlepoint and $q$ without solving the three equations; studentized_quantile searches along it.

source
SaddlepointExpansions.studentized_quantile — Method
studentized_quantile(K::CGF, prob; order=3, tol=1e-10) -> (; q, saddle)

The quantile of the saddlepoint distribution function of order order (studentized_tailprob with lower = true): $P(Q ≤ q) =$ prob. The search runs over $\hat s$ along the saddlepoint locus (studentized_locus), where each trial point gives its $q$ and its distribution function directly, so that no saddlepoint equation is solved: a bracket in $\hat s$ from the origin, then the Illinois iteration.

source
SaddlepointExpansions.studentized_saddlepoint — Method
studentized_saddlepoint(K::CGF, q) -> (; s, t, v, q0)

The saddlepoint $(\hat s, \hat t, \hat v)$ of the expansions for $Q = X/\sqrt{Y}$ at q, the root of $K_s = q v$, $K_t = v^2$, $q s + 2 t v = 0$ with $\hat v > 0$, where K is the joint cumulant generating function of $(X, Y)$, $Y > 0$. It is found by Newton's method with continuation in q from $q_0 = E(X)/\{E(Y)\}^{1/2}$, where $\hat s = \hat t = 0$.

source
SaddlepointExpansions.studentized_tailprob — Method
studentized_tailprob(K::CGF, q; order=3, lower=false, saddle=nothing) -> (; F, saddle, status, info)

Saddlepoint expansions of orders 1:order of $P(Q ≥ q)$ for $Q = X/\sqrt{Y}$, $Y > 0$, from the joint cumulant generating function K of $(X, Y)$ (Theorem 3 of Bao, Broda and Kan): F[M] keeps the terms of orders $n^{-m/2}$, $m ≤ 2M - 1$, and has relative error $O(n^{-M})$; info.Fp[j+1], $j = 0, …, 2M - 1$, are the expansions with the terms up to $n^{-j/2}$. lower = true gives $P(Q ≤ q)$. The expansion is of Hauschildt-Robinson type in the variable $s$ after the $t$-integral is expanded by Laplace's method: with $\Psi(s) = \psi(s, t(s))$, $\psi_t(s, t(s)) = 0$, the leading term is $E_n\{H_0(\hat s)\bar\Phi(\hat u) + …\}$, and all Taylor coefficients of $\Psi$ and of the amplitudes $H_k$ at $\hat s$ are obtained from the Taylor polynomial of $K$ by Newton's method and composition on power series, exact to rounding. saddle may supply the saddlepoint ((; s, t, v)); info.kappa is the cancellation indicator.

Library of cumulant generating functions

SaddlepointExpansions.common_component_cgf — Function
common_component_cgf(m, lam=zeros(length(m))) -> CGF

$Q_j = X_j + X_0$, $j = 1, …, d$, with independent $X_i ∼ χ^2_{m_i}(λ_i)$, m = [m_1, …, m_d, m_0] and lam likewise: $K(t) = \sum_j K_j(t_j) + K_0(\sum_j t_j)$, the $K_i$ noncentral chi-squared cumulant generating functions (ncx2_cgf). The pairs of quadratic forms of Section 6.4 of the paper, and the trivariate example, are of this form.

source
SaddlepointExpansions.exponential_sums_cgf — Method
exponential_sums_cgf(A; n=1, shift=nothing) -> CGF

The mean of n independent copies of $A'U + ℓ$, where $U$ is a vector of independent unit exponential variables, A is $m × d$ and shift $= ℓ$ (default $0$): $K(t) = -n\sum_i\log(1 - a_i't/n) + ℓ't$, $a_i$ the rows of A; domain $a_i't < n$ for all $i$. With n = 1 this is the cumulant generating function of $A'U + ℓ$ itself; the examples of Wang (1990) and Kolassa and Li (2010) in the paper are of this form.

source
SaddlepointExpansions.gamma_cgf — Function
gamma_cgf(a, b=1) -> CGF

Gamma distribution with shape a and scale b (mean $ab$): $K(t) = -a\log(1-bt)$, $t < 1/b$. gamma_cgf(1, b) is the exponential distribution with mean b.

source
SaddlepointExpansions.ncx2_cgf — Function
ncx2_cgf(m, lam=0) -> CGF

Noncentral chi-squared $χ^2_m(λ)$: $K(t) = -(m/2)\log(1-2t) + λt/(1-2t)$, $t < 1/2$.

source
SaddlepointExpansions.normal_cgf — Method
normal_cgf(mu, Sigma) -> CGF

$N(μ, Σ)$: $K(t) = μ't + t'Σt/2$. The expansions are exact at order one.

source
SaddlepointExpansions.quadratic_forms_cgf — Method
quadratic_forms_cgf(Mats; Sigma=nothing, mu=nothing, scale=1, closedform=true) -> CGF

Joint cumulant generating function of the quadratic forms $Q_i = z'A_iz$, $i = 1, …, d$, Mats = [A_1, …, A_d] (symmetric $p × p$), in $z ∼ N(μ, Σ)$ (default standard normal):

\[K(t) = -\tfrac12\log\det(I - 2C) + ν'C(I-2C)^{-1}ν,\qquad C = \sum_i t_i\,Σ^{1/2}A_iΣ^{1/2},\quad ν = Σ^{-1/2}μ,\]

multiplied by scale (the cumulant generating function of the sum of scale independent copies when scale is an integer). The domain is the set where $I - 2C$ is positive definite.

With closedform = true (the default) the Taylor polynomials, including the whitened ones, are computed in closed form by the trace recursion of the paper in the whitened directions (the taylor option of CGF), so that large $p$ costs $p^3$ floating-point operations per product of matrices rather than per product of truncated polynomials. With closedform = false the function is differentiated by Taylor-mode differentiation through a Cholesky factorization written in generic arithmetic, at about $p^3/6$ polynomial products per evaluation.

source
SaddlepointExpansions.sincu — Method
sincu(x)

$\sin(x)/x$ (unnormalized; Base.sinc is $\sin(πx)/(πx)$), equal to one at zero, for numbers and Taylor polynomials, with the removable singularity handled as in sinhc.

source
SaddlepointExpansions.sinhc — Method
sinhc(x)

$\sinh(x)/x$, equal to one at zero, for numbers and Taylor polynomials; at a Taylor polynomial with zero constant term the series $\sum_m x^{2m}/(2m+1)!$ is used, so that the removable singularity causes no DomainError. The cumulant generating function of the uniform distribution on $[-1, 1]$ is $\log \mathrm{sinhc}(t)$, and the saddlepoint iteration starts at $t = 0$, where $\sinh(t)/t$ would fail. See also sincu and uniform_cgf.

source
SaddlepointExpansions.uniform_cgf — Function
uniform_cgf(a=-1, b=1) -> CGF

Uniform distribution on $[a, b]$: $K(t) = \log\mathrm{sinhc}\{(b-a)t/2\} + (a+b)t/2$, finite for all $t$ (sinhc handles $t = 0$).

source
SaddlepointExpansions.wishart_diag_cgf — Method
wishart_diag_cgf(V, n) -> CGF

The diagonal of a Wishart matrix $W ∼ W_d(n, V)$: $K(t) = -(n/2)\log\det(I - 2V^{1/2} \mathrm{diag}(t)V^{1/2})$, the cumulant generating function of $d$ quadratic forms $z'e_ie_i'z$ in $z ∼ N(0, V)$, summed over n independent copies (quadratic_forms_cgf with scale = n).

The derivative interface

SaddlepointExpansions.spa_saddle — Function
spa_saddle(Kfun, x, tau0=nothing) -> (tau, info)

Saddlepoint $K'(τ)=x$ of a cumulant generating function in any dimension d = length(x). Kfun(nu, t) returns the derivative of order nu (a d-tuple of integers) at t, NaN outside the domain. Newton's method with backtracking on the convex objective $K(τ)-τ'x$ (a step is accepted only if it stays in the domain and decreases the objective). Convergence is measured by the Newton decrement $δ=\{g'K''(τ)^{-1}g\}^{1/2}$, $g=K'(τ)-x$, which is invariant under linear changes of the units of the components of $X$; the Newton step is computed after scaling $K''$ to unit diagonal. A step is also rejected when the objective falls below its tangent plane at the current iterate, $f(τ+λs) < f(τ) + λg's$, when the directional derivative decreases along the step, $\{g(τ+λs)-g(τ)\}'s < 0$, or when the midpoint of the step is outside the domain: on the strip the objective is convex, so it lies above its tangent plane and its gradient is monotone, and a violation means the step has crossed a pole of $K$ into a region where its formula happens to be finite (as $-\log|1-t|$ is beyond $t=1$, where the derivative changes sign). These are necessary conditions, so they protect cumulant generating functions given without a domain predicate in the usual cases; the predicate remains the safe way. Once $δ ≤ 10^{-6}$ the iteration is in its quadratic phase: full Newton steps are taken without the objective test, which rounding errors in $K$ would otherwise make fail, until $δ ≤$ tol ($10^{-12}$, or $ε^{0.7}$ in other floating-point types) or for at most three steps (twelve). Before that phase the iteration is declared stalled, and an error is raised, only when a step no longer changes the iterate in the working arithmetic or the backtracking finds no decrease; there is no threshold on the size of the step in the units of $x$. Components of $τ$ with $|τ_j|K''_{jj}^{1/2}$ below tol are set to zero, so that a saddlepoint on a coordinate hyperplane is not reported with a spurious sign, but only if the decrement at the rounded point is at most $\max(\mathrm{tol},2δ)$. The gradient, Hessian and decrement are recomputed at the point actually returned, and the result is accepted if that decrement is at most $10^{-6}$ (an error is raised otherwise). info holds the number of iterations, the decrement and the residual $K'(τ)-x$ at the returned point, tol, and snapped (the components set to zero). All tests are made in the working precision.

SaddlepointExpansions.spa_ratio_pdf — Method
spa_ratio_pdf(KWd, gd, shat, order) -> Vector

Rice (Laplace) expansion of orders 1:order of $(2\pi i)^{-1}\int \exp\{K_W(s)\}g(s)\,ds$ through the saddlepoint shat of $K_W$ ($K_W'(\hat s)=0$), Corollary 5 of the paper: KWd[k+1] = $K_W^{(k)}(\hat s)$, k = 0..2order, and gd[l+1] = $g^{(l)}(\hat s)$, l = 0..2order-2. For the density of $R=X/Y$ at r, with $Y>0$ and joint cumulant generating function $K(s,t)$, take $K_W(s)=K(s,-rs)$ and $g(s)=\partial K(s,t)/\partial t$ at $t=-rs$.

SaddlepointExpansions.spa_uni — Method
spa_uni(Kd, shat, xbar, order; uthr=2.5) -> (; pdf, cdf, upp, kappa)

Saddlepoint approximations of orders 1:order of the density and the distribution function of a scalar variable X at xbar, from the derivatives of its cumulant generating function at the saddlepoint: Kd[k+1] = $K^{(k)}(\hat s)$, k = 0,…,2order+1, with $K'(\hat s)=\bar x$.

  • pdf[M]: Daniels expansion with the terms m ≤ M-1 (relative error $O(n^{-M})$);
  • cdf[M]: Hauschildt-Robinson expansion of $P(X ≤ \bar x)$ with the terms m ≤ 2M-1;
  • upp[M]: the same expansion of $P(X > \bar x)$, computed directly (use it for small upper tails);
  • kappa[M]: cancellation indicator, (sum of |terms|)/|sum|.

Both signs of shat are handled (the reflection of Corollary 3). The "n" is absorbed in K.

The computation is generic in the floating-point type of the inputs. For Float64 the integrals $I_m(u)$, $u=\hat s K''(\hat s)^{1/2}$, are generated by the forward recursion (39) for |u| ≤ uthr and by 80-point Gauss-Hermite quadrature of a representation with positive integrands otherwise. For other types (e.g. BigFloat) the forward recursion is used at every u; it loses about $\log_{10}(u^{2m+1}/m!)$ digits at index m, so the precision must exceed that loss by the accuracy wanted.

orthant2(t, S)

$P(Z_1 ≥ t_1, Z_2 ≥ t_2)$, $Z ∼ N(0,S)$, by one-dimensional adaptive quadrature in the working precision.

SaddlepointExpansions.spa_bi — Method
spa_bi(Kfun, x, order; tau0=nothing, gamfun=nothing, nG=200, Imethod=:auto, Kwhite=nothing,
       gamwhite=nothing, direct=false, logscale=0, qtol=1e-8, nGmax=4nG)
    -> (; pdf, Fup, tau, canc, kappa, qerr, status, nG, coef_err_pdf, coef_err_cdf, saddle)

Bivariate saddlepoint approximations of orders 1:order at the point x = [x1, x2]. Kfun(nu, t) returns the derivative of order nu = (nu1, nu2) (a tuple of integers) of the cumulant generating function $K$ at t = [t1, t2]; nu = (0, 0) gives $K(t)$. It must return NaN outside the domain of $K$.

  • pdf[M]: density expansion with the terms m ≤ M-1 (relative error $O(n^{-M})$);
  • Fup[M]: $P(X_1 ≥ x_1, X_2 ≥ x_2)$ with the terms m ≤ 2M-1, valid when the saddlepoint tau is in the closed positive orthant (NaN otherwise; use spa_orthant2);
  • tau: the saddlepoint, found by spa_saddle (Newton's method with backtracking, convergence measured by the Newton decrement; an error is raised if it does not converge);
  • canc[M]: the largest |term| among the terms of Fup[M] divided by $I(0)$;
  • kappa[M]: (sum of |terms| of Fup[M])/|sum|; about $\log_{10}κ$ digits are lost to cancellation in the final sum (it does not measure errors in the coefficients, see coef_err_cdf);
  • qerr[M]: quadrature check of Fup[M]: its relative change with half the nodes, all retained terms included (0 for the exact method); while qerr[order] > qtol the number of nodes of the Gauss-Legendre rule is doubled, up to nGmax. A comparison of two quadratures, not a bound. Fup and qerr are NaN when the leading integral $I(0)$ cannot be resolved by the quadrature (it comes out zero or not finite), instead of a misleading zero;
  • status: "ok" or one or more of (joined by "; ") "negative saddlepoint component (use spa_orthant2)", "leading integral not resolved", "integration not resolved", "coefficients inaccurate" (a coefficient-error estimate above $10^{-6}$), "probability below the double-precision range (use logscale)" (the Chernoff bound $e^{K(τ)-τ'x-c}$ is below floatmin; Fup is then zero);
  • nG: the number of Gauss-Legendre nodes used;
  • coef_err_pdf[M], coef_err_cdf[M] (Float64 with the whitened evaluation; NaN otherwise): estimates of the relative errors of pdf[M] and Fup[M] caused by rounding in the formation of the whitened Taylor coefficients (first-order propagation of random perturbations of the size of the rounding errors of the substitution; with Kwhite only the rounding of the supplied derivatives);
  • saddle: the diagnostics of spa_saddle.

With gamfun, pdf is the density of a pair of ratios (Section 4): gamfun(tau) returns a tuple (G0, G1) of the Taylor coefficients at tau of the amplitude functions $g_0$, $g_1$, Gk[mu1+1, mu2+1] the coefficient of $t^μ$ in $g_k(τ+t)$, which enters $b_j(g_k)$ of Corollary 1, $|μ| ≤ 2(\mathrm{order}-1)$, and x should be [0, 0].

The arithmetic is done in the floating-point type of x (and tau0); pass BigFloat values (and a Kfun that is accurate in that precision) for variable precision. Imethod selects the computation of the integrals $I(ν)$: :quad (default for Float64; Gauss-Hermite or Gauss-Legendre quadrature with nG nodes per coordinate, Appendix B) or :exact (default for other types; binomial sum (14) and recursion of Lemma 2, which cancel in double precision).

Whitened evaluation (:quad). With $K''(τ)=LL'$ (Cholesky) and $T=L^{-T}$, the terms of each order are evaluated for $Y=L^{-1}X$: the Taylor coefficients of $K_Y(u)=K(Tu)$ at $\hat u=L'τ$ replace those of $K$ at $τ$, the Hermite polynomials $He_ν(w,K'')$ are replaced by products of univariate ones in $v=L^{-1}w$, and the orthant integrals $I(ν)$ by integrals of these products over the same orthant, at the same quadrature nodes. The sums of the terms of a given order are the same in exact arithmetic; in the whitened form the terms cancel much less when the components of $X$ are strongly correlated under the tilted distribution (Appendix B of the paper), provided the whitened coefficients are formed accurately. The method :exact keeps the original coordinates.

Keywords:

  • Kwhite: optional function (nu, u, T) -> derivative of order nu at u of $K_Y(u)=K(Tu)$, the cumulant generating function of $Y=T'X$, for a $2×2$ matrix T (with $T=L^{-T}$, $Y=L^{-1}X$); when absent, the Taylor coefficients of $K_Y$ are obtained from those of $K$ by polynomial substitution, which loses digits when $K''$ is ill conditioned (coef_err_pdf, coef_err_cdf estimate the loss);
  • gamwhite: optional function (u, T) -> (G0, G1), the amplitude coefficients of gamfun expressed in the whitened variable (otherwise substitution);
  • direct = true evaluates the orthant expansion also when tau has negative components, by the orthant-integral form of $I(ν)$ (the analytic continuation of the formula; the "direct" evaluation of Section 3.3, which has the wrong exponent; use spa_orthant2 for the probability itself);
  • logscale = c returns Fup scaled by $e^{-c}$, for probabilities beyond the range of double precision;
  • qtol, nGmax: tolerance and limit of the node escalation (see qerr).

In the Gauss-Legendre branch the exponential factor $E_n$ of (12) is combined with the normal density at the nodes in the log domain, and the density prefactor is formed from the logarithms of the diagonal of the Cholesky factor of $K''$, so that the products are formed without overflow or underflow of their factors. The nodes in $z_1$ are split, with nG nodes on each piece, at the edges of the boundary layer that the truncation $z_2 ≥ t_2$ creates when the correlation is near $±1$ and where the density of $z_1$ decays quickly because $t_1$ is far in its tail.

SaddlepointExpansions.spa_mv — Method
spa_mv(Kfun, x, order; tau0=nothing, method=:auto, refine=true, nG=64, nGH=48, nqmc=2^14,
       nshift=8, seed=1, Kwhite=nothing, qtol=nothing, nGmax=nothing)
    -> (; pdf, Fup, tau, kappa, err, kappa_pdf, refined, info)

Saddlepoint approximations of orders 1:order in dimension d = length(x) of the density (Theorem 3) and of the orthant probability $P(X ≥ x)$ (Theorem 1). Kfun(nu, t) returns the derivative of order nu (a d-tuple of integers) of the cumulant generating function at t (NaN outside the domain).

  • pdf[M]: density expansion with the terms m ≤ M-1, a finite sum evaluated without integrals (not the exact density);
  • Fup[M]: $P(X ≥ x)$ with the terms m ≤ 2M-1, when the saddlepoint tau has no negative component (NaN otherwise; use spa_orthant);
  • kappa[M], kappa_pdf[M]: cancellation indicators of the final sums Fup[M] and pdf[M] (sum of absolute values of the terms over the absolute value of the sum); they do not measure errors in the coefficients (see coef_err_pdf, coef_err_cdf);
  • err[M]: error estimate of Fup[M] from the integrals $I(ν)$, for every order: the change with half the quadrature nodes (:quad) or the standard error over random shifts (:qmc); NaN when no estimate is available (:exact, whose base probabilities come from adaptive quadrature). These compare two quadratures, are not bounds, and concern the evaluation of the retained terms only, not the asymptotic error;
  • info: named tuple with kappa_pdf; method_cdf, the method actually used for Fup; refined_pdf, refined_cdf, refined, digits and precision (the working precision of the returned values; see refine); tau_refined; saddle (see spa_saddle); nodes (the quadrature or QMC settings actually used); coef_err_pdf[M], coef_err_cdf[M]: estimates, in double precision, of the relative errors of pdf[M] and Fup[M] caused by rounding in the formation of the whitened Taylor coefficients (first-order propagation of random perturbations of the size of the rounding errors of the substitution; with Kwhite only the rounding of the supplied derivatives is included; NaN in BigFloat); and status: "ok" or one or more of (joined by "; ") "negative saddlepoint component (use spa_orthant)", "leading integral not resolved" (Fup and err are then NaN), "integration not resolved" ($err[order] >$ qtol $|Fup[order]|$ after the node escalation), "coefficients inaccurate" (a coefficient-error estimate above $10^{-6}$ that no refinement removed), "probability below the double-precision range" (the Chernoff bound $e^{K(τ)-τ'x}$ is below floatmin; Fup is then zero), "no orthant probability requested", "refinement failed" or "no error estimate".

The integrals $I(ν)$ of the orthant probability are computed by method:

  • :quad (d = 3, default there): Gauss-Hermite quadrature of the representation of Appendix B with nGH nodes per coordinate when $τ_j\{(Σ^{-1})_{jj}\}^{-1/2} > 2.5$ for all j, and otherwise Gauss-Legendre quadrature of the orthant-integral form with nG nodes per coordinate and piece in conditioned coordinates, the ranges of $z_1$ and $z_2$ split at the boundary layers created by correlations near $±1$ and at fast tail decays; while $err[order] >$ qtol $|Fup[order]|$ (qtol default $10^{-8}$, $10^{-3}$ for :qmc) the number of nodes is doubled, up to nGmax (default 2nG);
  • :qmc (default for d ≥ 4): randomized quasi-Monte Carlo (Richtmyer lattice, antithetic, nshift random shifts of nqmc points) after Genz's sequential conditioning of the truncated normal;
  • :exact (d = 3, default for BigFloat; also :recursion): binomial sum (14) and recursion of Lemma 2 in the working precision, with the normal orthant probabilities of dimensions two and three by adaptive quadrature in the working precision (so more digits do not by themselves make them more accurate).

In the double-precision methods the exponential factor $E_n$ of (12) is combined with the normal density or the probabilities of the sequential conditioning in the log domain, and the density prefactor is formed from the logarithms of the diagonal of the Cholesky factor of $K''$, so that the products are formed without overflow or underflow of their factors.

Whitened evaluation. With $K''=LL'$ (Cholesky) and $T=L^{-T}$, the terms of each order are evaluated for $Y=L^{-1}X$: the Taylor coefficients of $K_Y(u)=K(Tu)$ at $L'τ$ replace those of $K$ at $τ$, the Hermite polynomials $He_ν(w,K'')$ are replaced by products of univariate ones in $L^{-1}w$, and the integrals $I(ν)$ by integrals of these products over the same orthant, at the same nodes (in the frequency-domain representation $(iW)^ν$ becomes $(iV)^μ$, $W=TV$). The sums of the terms of a given order are unchanged in exact arithmetic, and the terms cancel much less when the components are strongly correlated under the tilted distribution, provided the whitened coefficients are formed accurately. Kwhite is an optional function (nu, u, T) -> derivative of order nu at u of $K_Y(u)=K(Tu)$, the cumulant generating function of $Y=T'X$, for a $d×d$ matrix T; without it the Taylor coefficients of $K_Y$ are obtained by polynomial substitution, whose loss of accuracy coef_err estimates. The method :exact keeps the original coordinates, since the recursion of Lemma 2 is tied to the orthant.

refine (default true): in more than two dimensions the terms of both expansions can cancel by many orders of magnitude at high orders, and the substitution can lose digits; when a double-precision cancellation indicator exceeds $10^6$, or a coefficient-error estimate exceeds $10^{-10}$, the computation is repeated in BigFloat with about $\log_{10}κ+26$ digits ($κ$ replaced by the estimate divided by eps() when that is larger), the derivatives of $K$ and hence the coefficients being computed in that precision: the density always, and for d = 3 with the quadrature method also the orthant probability (:exact). If this fails (for instance because Kfun does not accept BigFloat arguments), the double-precision values are kept, with a warning and info.status = "refinement failed".

qf_Kmulti(theta, Mats, L; Sinv=nothing, logdetSigma=nothing, maxidx=nothing)
    -> Dict{NTuple{p,Int},T} or nothing

Derivatives at theta of the joint cumulant generating function of the quadratic forms $z'A_iz$, $i=1,…,p$ (Mats = [A_1, …, A_p], symmetric), for all multi-indices $α$ with $|α| ≤ L$:

\[K(θ) = -\tfrac12\log\det(I-2C),\quad C=\sum_iθ_iA_i,\qquad K_α = \frac{2^{|α|-1}α!}{|α|}\operatorname{tr}F_α,\quad F_α=\sum_i DA_iF_{α-e_i}, \quad D=(I-2C)^{-1},\]

The result is a Dict keyed by the multi-index as a tuple, e.g. K[(1, 0, 2)]; K[(0, 0, 0)] is $K(θ)$. Returns nothing outside the domain ($I-2C$ not positive definite). maxidx (a tuple) restricts the multi-indices to $α ≤$ maxidx componentwise. The traces are formed as $\operatorname{tr}F_α=\sum_{|β|=⌊|α|/2⌋}\operatorname{tr}(F_βF_{α-β})$, so that only the $F_β$ with $|β| ≤ ⌈L/2⌉$ are formed. Generic in the element type of the matrices (e.g. BigFloat).

SaddlepointExpansions.wang_bivar — Method

Module

SaddlepointExpansions — Module
SaddlepointExpansions

Saddlepoint expansions of any order for tail probabilities and densities of random vectors, from the cumulant generating function alone. The expansions are those of Broda and Kan, "Uniform saddlepoint expansions for multivariate tail probabilities": the orthant probability $P(X ≥ x)$ (Theorem 1, with the inclusion-exclusion of Section 3.3 for saddlepoints with negative components), the density (Theorem 3) and the density and distribution function of a ratio (Theorem 4 and Corollaries), in any dimension, together with the univariate expansions of Daniels and of Hauschildt and Robinson as the case $d = 1$.

The user supplies the cumulant generating function $K$ as an ordinary Julia function of a vector, wrapped in a CGF. All derivatives of $K$ that the expansions need are generated by Taylor-mode automatic differentiation (TaylorSeries.jl), directly in the whitened coordinates in which the terms are evaluated, so that no derivative formulas have to be written by hand. The main functions are tailprob, density, ratio_density, ratio_tailprob, saddlepoint and derivatives, and, for a Studentized mean $X/\sqrt{Y}$ (Bao, Broda and Kan, "Inversion formulas and saddlepoint expansions for Studentized means"), studentized_density, studentized_tailprob and studentized_quantile. Ready-made cumulant generating functions for common models are in the library (normal_cgf, gamma_cgf, ncx2_cgf, quadratic_forms_cgf, wishart_diag_cgf, exponential_sums_cgf, common_component_cgf, uniform_cgf), and cumulant generating functions can be transformed (reflect, marginal, linear_combination, sample_mean).

Everything is generic in the floating-point type: pass BigFloat arguments inside setprecision for variable precision. The lower-level functions spa_uni, spa_bi, spa_mv, spa_orthant, ... take the derivatives of $K$ from a user-supplied function Kfun(nu, t) instead, for cumulant generating functions whose derivatives are available in closed form.
